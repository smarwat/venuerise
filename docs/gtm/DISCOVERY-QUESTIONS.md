# Discovery Questions

**Purpose:** surface real revenue leaks in the venue's current process so the demo lands on actual pain (not generic pitch).

**Rule:** ask, then **shut up.** The most valuable thing you do on a discovery call is let the prospect describe their own broken workflow in their own words. Their words become your closing language later.

---

## Lead volume

- How many wedding inquiries does the venue typically get per month?
- How seasonal is your demand? When are your peak inquiry months?
- What percentage of inquiries feel like serious couples vs window-shoppers?
- Has lead volume been growing, flat, or declining over the last year?

---

## Lead sources

- Where do most of your wedding inquiries come from?
  - Website form?
  - The Knot?
  - WeddingWire?
  - Instagram DMs?
  - Meta Ads?
  - Google Ads?
  - Referrals (planners, photographers, past clients)?
  - Email / phone direct?
- What's your annual spend on paid sources (The Knot, WeddingWire, Google, Meta)?
- Which of those feels like it produces the best couples?
- Which feels like it produces the worst?

---

## Response workflow

- Who replies when an inquiry comes in?
- How quickly do you usually respond? Minutes? Hours? Next business day?
- Do you use reply templates or write from scratch each time?
- How often do inquiries get missed entirely?
- What happens to inquiries that come in after hours / on weekends?
- Who covers replies when your main person is in a tasting / event / off?

---

## Tour workflow

- What does it take for an inquiry to become a tour booking?
- How many tours do you typically run per month?
- What's your no-show rate on scheduled tours?
- Who confirms tours? When?
- What follow-up happens after a tour wraps?
- How many days does the average couple take to decide after touring?

---

## Current tools

- What CRM are you using? (HoneyBook? Tripleseat? Aisle Planner? Planning Pod? a spreadsheet?)
- Where do tours live — the CRM's calendar, Google Calendar, somewhere else?
- How are inquiries flowing into your CRM today — manual entry, form integration, copy-paste?
- Any automations running today? (auto-replies, drip sequences, etc.)
- Do you use any AI/chat tools today for inquiry handling?

---

## Revenue economics

- What's your average booking value for a wedding?
- What's your deposit/retainer size?
- What's your close rate from a tour? (out of 10 tours, how many become signed contracts?)
- How many extra weddings per quarter would meaningfully impact the business?

---

## Pain validation (the real ones)

Ask, then listen:

- Which part of the inquiry-to-booking process feels most broken right now?
- If you could fix one thing about the way you handle leads, what would it be?
- Have you ever lost a wedding to a competitor where you suspect it was because your team was slower to reply?
- When a lead goes quiet for 2–3 weeks, what usually happens?
- Can you tell me, off the top of your head, which inquiry source produced your last 5 booked weddings? *(Most can't. The moment they hesitate is the moment they realize they have an attribution problem.)*
- What does your team do when they hit max capacity for follow-ups?

---

## Buying process

- Who would be involved in deciding whether to pilot a tool like this?
- Do you have budget set aside for sales tools / sales ops, or would this be a new line item?
- What's your usual timeline for trying something new — weeks, months?
- Any existing contracts (CRM, lead-gen services) you're locked into?
- Would you be open to a 30-day paid pilot if we can show you measurable leakage in your current pipeline?

---

## Qualification scoring

Use these answers to bucket the venue:

### A-tier venue (close priority)

- 40+ wedding inquiries / month
- Average booking value $10k+
- 3+ active lead sources (at least one paid)
- Owner / GM / sales director is in the conversation
- They named a specific follow-up or attribution pain in their own words
- They confirmed budget OR they confirmed a pilot is worth exploring
- They're not currently locked into a competing tool that solves the same problem

### B-tier venue (nurture; demo if calendar allows)

- 20–40 inquiries / month, OR average booking value $5k–$10k
- 2 lead sources
- Decision-maker not yet in the conversation (talking to coordinator)
- Some pain mentioned but no urgency
- Open to a demo but not ready to commit to a pilot

### Bad fit (politely close out)

- < 15 inquiries / month
- Average booking value < $5k
- Only one lead source (e.g. word-of-mouth only)
- Owner explicitly says "we don't have a follow-up problem"
- Fully booked and not interested in optimization
- Asking for fully autonomous AI that sends without human approval
- Demanding partnerships (The Knot official partner status, etc.) before they'll try the product

For bad-fit venues, thank them, leave the door open ("If your inquiry workflow changes in the future, I'm here"), and move on. **Do not run demos for bad-fit venues** — it burns your time and you'll close 0%.

---

## What to do with the answers

After every discovery call, drop the answers into your CRM / tracking sheet in this format:

```
Venue: [name]
Date: [yyyy-mm-dd]
Contact: [name + role]
Tier: A / B / bad fit
Lead volume: ~X/month
Sources: [list]
Pain in their words: "[exact quote]"
Average booking: $X
Decision-maker engaged: yes / no
Pilot openness: open / curious / not now / no
Next step: [demo on date / follow up on date / closed-lost]
```

The "pain in their words" field is the most valuable. After 10 calls, you'll see 3–4 phrases repeated — those become the headlines of your next email sequence.
