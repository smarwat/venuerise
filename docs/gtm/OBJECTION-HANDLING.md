# Objection Handling

For every objection, the move is the same:

1. **Acknowledge** the concern (don't argue with it)
2. **Reframe** what VenueRise actually does (it's usually different from what they're objecting to)
3. **Ask a question** to keep the conversation going

Never argue. Never get defensive. The buyer's objection is information — use it.

---

## "We already use HoneyBook / Tripleseat / Planning Pod / [a CRM]"

**Response:**

> That makes sense — and a lot of the venues we work with use one of those. VenueRise isn't trying to replace them. Your CRM stores the lead. VenueRise shows which leads are leaking revenue right now and helps your team take the next action faster. Different jobs.
>
> Out of curiosity — does your current CRM tell you which qualified leads don't have a tour booked yet, or which inquiries from The Knot vs Instagram are becoming booked weddings?

**Why this works:** repositions the product as additive, not replacement. Then asks a question their CRM probably can't answer, which surfaces the real pain.

---

## "We already get enough leads"

**Response:**

> That's actually exactly why this matters. The goal isn't more leads — it's converting more of the leads you already paid for into tours and bookings. If you're already at full inquiry volume, then the bigger lever is making sure the high-fit ones don't get lost in the noise.
>
> How confident are you that every qualified inquiry from last month got a same-day reply and a tour offer?

---

## "AI sounds robotic"

**Response:**

> Totally fair — and that's why VenueRise is approval-based. The AI drafts using your venue's profile, brand voice, and knowledge base, but your team reads and approves every reply before it goes out. Nothing gets sent on its own.
>
> If a draft sounds robotic, your coordinator just edits it. Over time, the system learns your team's style.

---

## "We don't want AI sending messages on our behalf"

**Response:**

> It doesn't have to. The default workflow is: AI drafts, your team approves and sends. Manual channels like Instagram and The Knot stay manual — VenueRise drafts the reply, but your coordinator copies it and sends from inside those platforms.
>
> The point of the AI isn't to take over the conversation. It's to save your team the 5 minutes per reply they spend writing from scratch.

---

## "We're fully booked already"

**Response:**

> Then the value is probably less about more bookings and more about three other things:
>
> First, prioritization — when you're at capacity, you want to make sure the best-fit leads get your best time, not whoever happens to email first.
>
> Second, team consistency — when a high-fit couple inquires while your coordinator is in a tasting, the system makes sure the reply still goes out within an hour, not the next day.
>
> Third, source visibility — knowing which sources actually produce premium bookings means you can shift spend to those and stop paying for the noisy ones.
>
> Does any of that resonate?

---

## "We don't have time to set this up"

**Response:**

> Which is exactly why the pilot is services-assisted. We don't drop you a login and disappear. We configure the venue profile, the lead sources, the knowledge base, the tour availability, and the brand voice with you over the first week.
>
> Most venues are operating the daily workflow inside the first week. Your team's time investment is roughly one 60-min setup call, then 15 minutes a day reviewing drafts.

---

## "How do I know it works?"

**Response:**

> Fair — and I'm not going to claim it's guaranteed to work for every venue. What I can tell you is the pilot is structured to measure leakage specifically: slow replies, stale leads, qualified leads without a tour, unconfirmed tours, and source-to-booked outcomes.
>
> After 30 days, you'll have a clear picture of whether those leaks exist in your process and whether catching them produced more booked weddings. If the answer is no, you'd be a clear renew-no for us — and that's fine.

---

## "What does it cost?"

**Flexible response:**

> For early pilots, pricing depends on lead volume and setup complexity. The way I usually frame it: if one recovered wedding booking is worth $10k–$25k to you, the pilot only needs to help you recover a small number of opportunities to justify the cost.
>
> The starter pilot is in the low four figures per month. Want me to walk through what's included so we can talk through what fits your situation?

**Internal note:** see `docs/gtm/PILOT-OFFER.md` for actual pricing tiers. Don't drop a specific number too early — anchor on value first, then quote.

---

## "Can you integrate directly with The Knot / WeddingWire / Instagram?"

**Response:**

> Depends on the channel.
>
> Website inquiries and email — fully automatic.
>
> Instagram DMs — Meta has an official API. We're going through their app review process now. Until that's approved, Instagram replies stay in the operator-drafted workflow.
>
> The Knot and WeddingWire — there's no public outbound API on either platform. So VenueRise drafts the reply, your coordinator copies and sends from inside the platform's inbox. The system still tracks that the reply went out so the team and the dashboard stay in sync.
>
> I'd rather be honest about what's direct integration vs operator-drafted than pretend everything is fully automated. Most of the alternatives in this space aren't honest about it.

---

## "Is this guaranteed to increase bookings?"

**Response:**

> No. I won't claim that. If a salesperson tells you yes, run.
>
> What I can do is show you where revenue is currently leaking — slow replies, stale leads, unbooked tours, sources you're spending on that aren't producing bookings — and help your team act on those before leads go cold.
>
> Whether that produces more bookings depends on your lead volume, your team's process, and your booking economics. The pilot is structured to measure that, not to promise it.

**Response:**

> That's increasingly common — there's a lot of half-baked AI in this space right now. What was the specific frustration?
>
> [Listen.]
>
> The most common ones I hear: the AI sent something embarrassing, the AI replaced human judgment, or the AI was great in the demo and broken in production. VenueRise is built to fail in the opposite direction — every reply is operator-approved, nothing autonomous, and the dashboard is structured so you can see exactly what the AI suggested and what your team did with it.

> Happy to send a proposal. So I write the right one — are we talking about the starter pilot, or do you need a custom scope?
>
> [If they're vague:] Let me send you the standard pilot proposal with the three tiers. If one feels close to right we can fine-tune. If none fit, we can talk through a custom scope.

Send within 24 hours, max. Use the template in `docs/gtm/POST-DEMO-FOLLOW-UP.md`.

## What to never do

- **Never argue.** Even when you know the objection is wrong, arguing makes you wrong.
- **Never overpromise to overcome an objection.** "We're SOC 2 certified" / "We guarantee 3× more tours" closes one objection and opens a much worse one (lawsuit, broken trust).
- **Never trash competitors by name.** "HoneyBook is bad at this" lowers the trust the buyer has in you.
- **Never pitch features as the response to an emotional objection.** "We had a bad experience" doesn't get fixed by "well, we have brand voice calibration."
- **Never end the call without a defined next step.** Even "I'll call you in 3 months" is a next step. "I'll think about it" is not.

## Tracking objections

After every call, log the objection you heard in your tracking sheet:

```
Objection: [exact quote]
Type: [CRM / AI distrust / pricing / timing / capacity / autonomy / authority / other]
How you responded:
Outcome:
```

After 20 calls you'll see the same 3–5 objections repeated. Those go in the next iteration of your email copy and demo intro so future prospects don't even raise them.
