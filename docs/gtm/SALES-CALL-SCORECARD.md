# Sales Call Scorecard

Score every discovery / demo call **immediately after** it ends. Don't wait until the end of the day — your memory of the signals fades fast.

Use this to:

1. Decide whether to invest more time in this prospect
2. Track which conversations correlate with closed pilots over time
3. Spot patterns in your own questioning (e.g. you keep skipping the budget conversation)

---

## Scoring categories (10 points each, 100 total)

### 1. Lead volume (10 pts)

| Signal | Points |
|---|---|
| 60+ inquiries / month | 10 |
| 30–60 / month | 7 |
| 15–30 / month | 4 |
| < 15 / month | 1 |
| Don't know / wouldn't say | 2 |

### 2. Channel fragmentation (10 pts)

| Signal | Points |
|---|---|
| 4+ active lead sources (incl. at least 2 paid) | 10 |
| 3 sources | 7 |
| 2 sources | 4 |
| 1 source (e.g. only website, or only word-of-mouth) | 1 |

### 3. Response-time pain (10 pts)

| Signal | Points |
|---|---|
| Owner explicitly named slow follow-up as a problem in their own words | 10 |
| Owner agreed it was an issue when asked | 6 |
| Owner said "we're fine" but team admitted otherwise | 4 |
| Owner said "we're fine" and seemed confident | 1 |

### 4. Tour conversion pain (10 pts)

| Signal | Points |
|---|---|
| Owner knows their tour close rate and named it as a problem | 10 |
| Owner roughly knows close rate, sees room for improvement | 7 |
| Owner doesn't know close rate but is curious | 5 |
| Owner says "we book everyone who tours" (probably untrue) | 2 |

### 5. Owner / decision-maker urgency (10 pts)

| Signal | Points |
|---|---|
| Owner / GM / sales director is on the call AND named timing pressure | 10 |
| Decision-maker on call, no urgency named | 6 |
| Coordinator on call, would loop in owner | 4 |
| Coordinator on call, hasn't talked to owner about this | 2 |

### 6. Average booking value (10 pts)

| Signal | Points |
|---|---|
| > $25k average booking | 10 |
| $15k–$25k | 8 |
| $8k–$15k | 6 |
| $5k–$8k | 3 |
| < $5k | 0 |

### 7. Willingness to pilot (10 pts)

| Signal | Points |
|---|---|
| Asked about pricing / pilot scope unprompted | 10 |
| Said yes when asked about a paid pilot | 7 |
| Curious but non-committal | 5 |
| Pushed for free pilot or "just want to try the dashboard" | 3 |
| Explicit no | 0 |

### 8. Operational complexity (10 pts)

Higher complexity = higher value of the product (more sources, more team members, more dropped balls). Inversed slightly to reward complex venues.

| Signal | Points |
|---|---|
| Multi-coordinator team + 4+ sources + no central inbox | 10 |
| Multi-coordinator team OR multi-source | 7 |
| Single coordinator, multi-source | 5 |
| Single coordinator, 1–2 sources | 3 |

### 9. Decision-maker access (10 pts)

| Signal | Points |
|---|---|
| On the call as the decision-maker | 10 |
| Not on the call but confirmed they'll be involved | 7 |
| Not on the call, vague about access | 3 |
| Coordinator is gatekeeping the owner | 1 |

### 10. Budget fit (10 pts)

| Signal | Points |
|---|---|
| Confirmed budget OR explicitly open to four-figure pilot | 10 |
| Mentioned existing spend on lead-gen / sales tools | 7 |
| No mention of budget but no pushback when ranges discussed | 5 |
| Pushed back on price | 2 |
| Said explicitly "we have no budget" | 0 |

## Tier brackets

### A-tier (75+)

- Push hard. Send the proposal within 24 hours.
- Multiple follow-ups OK over 2 weeks
- These convert at ~25–40% to pilot

### B-tier (50–74)

- Send a follow-up but don't chase
- Nurture for 30–60 days
- These convert at ~5–15% to pilot, eventually

### Bad fit (< 50)

- Send a polite "stay in touch" email
- Add to 90-day quarterly check-in
- Move on — don't burn time pushing
- These convert at < 2% to pilot in the next 6 months

---

## Hard disqualifiers (auto bad-fit regardless of score)

Even if a venue scores 70+, treat them as bad-fit if **any** of:

- They demand fully autonomous AI sending as a precondition
- They demand "official partner" status with The Knot / WeddingWire / Meta
- They demand SOC 2 / GDPR certification on day one
- Average booking value < $4k AND inquiry volume < 20/month *(unit economics don't work)*
- They want to white-label / resell *(not the customer we're building for)*
- They have a competitor product they're under contract with for 6+ months
- They explicitly say "I want to replace my CRM entirely" *(VenueRise is additive, not replacement)*

## Weekly scorecard review

Every Friday, look at the scorecards from the week:

1. **Average score:** if your average is < 40, your list is wrong (you're talking to the wrong venues, not selling badly)
2. **A-tier conversion rate:** if A-tier prospects aren't converting to pilots, your demo or follow-up is the problem — not the targeting
3. **Bad-fit volume:** if more than 50% are bad-fit, tighten the qualification on cold outreach so you stop wasting time
4. **Hard disqualifier frequency:** if you're seeing the same disqualifier repeatedly (e.g. everyone wants SOC 2), that's a real signal — flag it for product/positioning

The scorecard is more useful for **your own learning** than as a CRM artifact. Don't treat it as paperwork. Treat it as the place you become a better seller.
