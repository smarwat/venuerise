# GTM Playbook

The operating manual for taking VenueRise to market.

---

## ICP — Ideal Customer Profile

### A-tier (close priority)

- **Property type:** wedding / event venue (estate, ballroom, garden, barn, boutique hotel with wedding business)
- **Inquiry volume:** 30+ wedding inquiries / month
- **Average booking value:** $8k+ (most A-tiers are $12k–$30k)
- **Lead sources:** 3+ active, at least one paid (The Knot, WeddingWire, Google Ads, Meta Ads)
- **Team:** owner or GM is directly involved in sales, with a coordinator (or small sales team) handling daily follow-up
- **Tech posture:** has some kind of CRM or shared inbox but knows it's not optimized for wedding inquiry workflow
- **Geography:** US-based initially (timezone + language + payment friction)
- **Business stage:** established (1+ year of operations), not pre-launch

### B-tier (nurture)

- 15–30 inquiries / month
- Average booking value $5k–$8k
- 2 sources
- Coordinator-led sales, owner not in the conversation yet
- Some pain mentioned but no urgency

### Bad fit (don't pitch)

- Tiny venues with low lead volume (< 15/month)
- Venues with no digital inquiries (walk-in / phone-only)
- Venues unwilling to change workflow
- Venues that only care about more ads, not conversion of existing leads
- Venues demanding fully autonomous AI before trust is built
- Venues asking for white-label / reseller arrangements
- Pre-launch or "we just opened" venues — pitch them in 6–12 months

## Where to find them

### Source lists (in order of effectiveness)

1. **The Knot listings** — every venue on The Knot is paying for a lead source, which is the strongest signal they care about inquiry conversion. Filter by region, capacity, category.
2. **WeddingWire listings** — same as above.
3. **Google Maps** — search "[city] wedding venue" — get name, website, sometimes phone. Manual but high quality.
4. **Instagram hashtags** — `#weddingvenue`, `#[city]weddingvenue`, `#weddingvenueowner`. Surface real operators.
5. **Wedding photographer / planner referrals** — they know which venues are well-run and which struggle with follow-up.
6. **Venue owner Facebook groups** — many regional groups exist. Lurk first; participate before pitching.
7. **Local wedding vendor networks / chambers of commerce.**

### Lists to AVOID

- Paid B2B lead lists (Apollo, ZoomInfo) — these have stale wedding venue data; the venue's "head of sales" listed there is usually the owner's spouse or a 5-year-old coordinator who's no longer there.
- LinkedIn scraping — wedding venue owners aren't on LinkedIn much.
- Generic event-tech directories.

---

## Outreach channels (in order of effectiveness for this ICP)

1. **Cold call** — wedding venue owners answer their phone. Highest connect rate of any channel.
2. **Instagram DM** — they live in Instagram. Voice notes outperform text DMs 2–3×.
3. **Cold email** — works at scale, lower conversion per send than calls but higher volume.
4. **Planner / photographer referrals** — slowest to build, highest conversion when warm.
5. **Owner Facebook groups** — only after you've earned a reputation in the group.
6. **Webinars / content** — defer until product is more proven; not the right play for early-stage outreach.

## Daily activity targets

### Early validation sprint (first 30 venues)

Per day:
- 30 venues researched (added to outreach list with verified contact)
- 20 cold calls dialed
- 20 cold emails sent
- 20 Instagram DMs sent
- 5 follow-ups (replies, second touches)
- **Target: 1–2 demos booked per day**

This pace burns out a solo founder fast. Either:
- Compress to 3 weeks of intense activity, then triage
- Hire a part-time SDR (US-based, $25–35/hr) after the first 5 pilots prove the offer works
- Use a sales tool (Apollo, Outreach, Lemlist) for email sequencing only — keep calls + DMs manual

### Sustained pace (after initial sprint)

Per day:
- 10 calls
- 10 emails
- 10 DMs
- 5–10 follow-ups
- 1 demo

## The 30 Venue Validation Sprint

**Goal:** 30 high-quality conversations with venues that fit the A-tier ICP, in 30 days.

**Outcomes to hit:**
- 30 discovery / demo conversations completed
- 10+ paid pilot proposals sent
- 3+ paid pilots signed

**If you hit those numbers:** you have product-market fit signal. Move to scale.

**If you don't:**

| Result | Likely problem |
|---|---|
| < 30 conversations | List / channel / outreach copy is wrong |
| 30 conversations but < 10 proposals sent | Demo isn't landing; the offer isn't clear |
| 10 proposals but < 3 signed | Pricing, scope, or pilot offer is wrong |
| 3+ signed but 0 renew at end of pilot | Product doesn't actually solve the leak (the real problem) |

Each failure mode points at a different fix. Don't conflate them.

## Weekly review (every Friday)

30-minute review with yourself (or your team). Look at:

1. **Which message got replies this week?** (subject lines, opening lines, DM hooks)
2. **Which objections came up most?**
3. **Which channels produced the most demos booked?**
4. **What pricing resistance did you hear?**
5. **What product gaps blocked sales?**
6. **Is the venue pain real, or are you imagining it?**

Update your copy + targeting based on the review. Don't wait a quarter — iterate weekly.

---

## When to add a second seller

Add an SDR / second seller when:

- You've signed 5+ pilots
- You've renewed at least 3 to retainer
- You have a documented playbook (cold call script, email sequence, demo script, objection handling) — i.e. **all the files in this folder**
- You can articulate the ICP in one sentence and someone else could repeat it

Don't hire before you've sold the first 5 yourself. The founder needs to feel the early pitch friction directly.

---

## When to expand beyond US wedding venues

After 25–50 paid customers in the US wedding vertical:

- **Adjacent verticals first:** corporate event venues, hotels with banquet revenue, museums with rental space. Similar inquiry-to-booking shape.
- **Geographic expansion:** UK + Canada are obvious next steps (English, similar wedding industry economics). Australia after that.
- **Vertical depth:** stay in venues. Don't try to be "AI Revenue OS for any business" — the whole thesis is built on wedding-specific workflow.

## What success looks like at each stage

| Stage | Customers | Signal |
|---|---|---|
| Validation | 1–5 paid pilots | "Some venues will pay for this" |
| Early traction | 5–15 paid retainers | "Pilots are renewing, the leak is real" |
| Repeatable sales | 15–30 paid | "The playbook works, not just the founder" |
| Scalable | 30–75 paid | "Second seller is producing similar numbers" |
| Default scale | 75+ paid | "Multi-channel + content + outbound all producing" |

The first inflection is **first paid pilot.** Until you have that, every other plan is theory.

## What can break the whole GTM

Risks to watch for, ranked by likelihood:

1. **Venues sign pilot but don't engage with the weekly review.** They paid, but didn't put in the team time. The leak doesn't get measured. They don't renew. *Fix:* gate access to onboarding on team commitment ("we need 60 min from your coordinator on day 1, plus 30 min/week — confirm before we start").

2. **The product looks good in demo but the dashboard is empty for the first 2 weeks of real customer use** (because new inquiries take time to show meaningful leakage). Customer churns out of impatience. *Fix:* day-1 setup includes backfill of recent leads + clear expectation-setting ("the leakage view fills out across the first 14 days").

3. **The Knot / WeddingWire integration gap kills the trust.** If a buyer has been pitched by competitors who claim full automation, the manual-required honesty positioning may feel weaker. *Fix:* keep leaning into the honesty differentiator. Buyers who've been burned by overpromising competitors actually love this — but you'll lose some sales to the overpromisers in the short term.

4. **Pricing is too high for the unit economics.** A $15k average booking venue with 25 inquiries/month doesn't have room to absorb $3k/mo in software. *Fix:* the starter tier exists for this reason. Don't push every venue into Growth.

5. **The founder gets pulled into product fires and stops selling.** Most common early failure. *Fix:* protect 2–3 hours/day for outbound. Calendar block.

## Recommended next phase: GTM-1 — 30 Venue Validation Sprint

After this phase ships, the next move is to **actually run the sprint.** Build a target list of 60–80 venues that fit the A-tier ICP (you'll lose 50% to non-responses), and start the outreach cadence above.

Track everything in the scorecard format. Run a weekly review. Iterate the cold email subject lines after week 1. Iterate the demo script after week 2.

The goal of GTM-1 isn't to close 30 customers. It's to have 30 real conversations so you can answer the question:

> "Do A-tier wedding venues, when shown a realistic Revenue OS demo on their inquiry pipeline, pay for a 30-day pilot to measure leakage?"

The answer to that question — yes, no, or *yes but only at $X price* — defines everything that comes after.
